\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Airborne software loaders exchange INFORMATION and UPLOAD traffic under declared
timers, file identities and operator actions~\cite{arinc615a3}. A missed or late
acknowledgement, a mismatched Load File, or an unresolved integrity check can
be a protocol failure, a measurement artefact, or an unconfirmed injection.
The engineering question is whether a later legal action can be chosen from
residual ambiguity under a finite resource.

This paper studies Closed-Loop Test--Analysis Verification (CL-TAV). Three
reader questions are used throughout:
\begin{itemize}
\item CL-RQ1 (loop consistency): do prediction, interpretation and candidate
update stay mutually consistent under one uncertainty reference?
\item CL-RQ2 (comparative effect): under the same budget and denominators, does
the closed loop change detection or isolation relative to Test-only,
Analysis-only and fixed Test-then-Analysis arms?
\item CL-RQ3 (uncertainty and stopping): how do interval timing, \texttt{ERROR},
Prep/Recover and exclusive stops affect those comparisons?
\end{itemize}

Two propositions are evaluated, not asserted as established results. C1 is
uncertainty-aware interpretation, timing verdicts and compatible update
(CL-RQ1). C2 is feeding residual ambiguity into the next exclusive action with
explicit resource, Prep/Recover, \texttt{ERROR} and stop rules (CL-RQ2/3).

Requirements-based generation organises stimuli from obligations~\cite{yang2025}.
Conformance relations such as \emph{ioco} generate tests from labelled
transition systems~\cite{tretmans1996}. FSM covering and mutant killing select
discriminating sequences~\cite{chow1978,fujiwara1991,petrenko2016,jia2011}.
Section~\ref{sec:related} compares those stated objects with CL-RQ1--CL-RQ3.
The comparison is not a proof that those methods cannot be extended.

The evaluation uses four arms (CL-T, CL-A, CL-TA, CL-LOOP) and three registered
families (DETECT, LOCATE, ABLATION). Confirmatory numbers are not available.
