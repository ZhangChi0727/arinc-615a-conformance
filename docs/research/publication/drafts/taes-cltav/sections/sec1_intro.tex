\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Airborne software loaders exchange INFORMATION and UPLOAD traffic under declared
timers, file identities and operator actions~\cite{arinc615a3}. Loading
frameworks also treat identity, integrity, interrupted loading and version
conflict as connected engineering concerns~\cite{marques2021}. A missed or late
acknowledgement, a mismatched Load File, or an unresolved integrity check can
therefore be product behaviour, a capture or clock limitation, a configuration
discrepancy, or an unconfirmed injection. The engineering problem is not merely
to collect more tests, but to decide whether the current evidence is usable and
which later protocol-legal action is worth its finite resource.

This paper studies Closed-Loop Test--Analysis Verification (CL-TAV). Three
reader questions are used throughout:
\begin{itemize}
\item CL-RQ1 (loop consistency): do prediction, interpretation and candidate
update stay mutually consistent under one uncertainty reference?
\item CL-RQ2 (comparative effect): under the same budget and denominators, does
the closed loop change detection or isolation relative to Test-only,
Analysis-only and fixed Test-then-Analysis arms?
\item CL-RQ3 (uncertainty and stopping): how do interval timing, \texttt{ERROR},
Prep/Recover and exclusive stops affect those comparisons?
\end{itemize}

Two propositions are evaluated, not asserted as established results. C1 is
mutual consistency of uncertainty-aware interpretation, timing verdict and
whole-history compatible update in the declared finite domain; its retention
statement is conditional on model, ownership, pairing and conservative
observation assumptions. C2 feeds residual ambiguity into a prior-free,
one-step worst-case candidate-set choice among feasible affordable tests, with
explicit Prep/Recover, \texttt{ERROR}, resource and stop rules. C2 is not a
global multi-step optimum and need not shrink the candidate set on every
outcome.

Requirements-based generation, conformance testing and mutation adequacy supply
important test-construction and evaluation tools~\cite{tretmans1996,petrenko2016,jia2011,yang2025}.
Active diagnosis already closes observation--control loops, while real-time
diagnosis and testing already address uncertain communication timing
\cite{sampath1998,hu2020,miao2025,koehl2023,fraenzle2026}. The narrower question
here is whether the declared 615A action, observation, cost and stop contracts
form a useful and empirically defensible combination. Section~\ref{sec:related}
therefore compares stated objects and assumptions; it does not infer novelty
from different terminology or prove that another method cannot be extended.

The evaluation uses four arms (CL-T, CL-A, CL-TA, CL-LOOP) and three registered
families (DETECT, LOCATE, ABLATION). Confirmatory numbers are not available.
